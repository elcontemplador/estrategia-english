Throughout history, leadership has been accompanied by the figure of the adviser: someone able to guide, inspire and broaden a leader's outlook at decisive moments. Today, the arrival of conversational artificial intelligence opens a new era in which “digital advisers” assume a similar role, but with cognitive capabilities enhanced by machine learning and natural language processing. This article reflects on the evolution of the concept of advising and examines how human–AI interaction redefines analysis, decision-making and action in contemporary leadership.

## 1. Decision before action

Humanity's great transformations have grown out of decisions preceded by reflection. Every consequential action began as a considered decision.

This sequence — deciding before acting — marks the difference between the impulsive and the momentous. At the root of every good decision, there is usually a conversation: a dialogue with oneself, or with someone whose wisdom illuminates the possible paths.

Here the adviser emerges, not as someone who dictates what must be done, but as someone who helps us think better. In the biographies of great leaders, from politicians and scientists to philosophers and artists, we always find someone who served as an intellectual and emotional mirror.

## 2. The adviser in the history of leadership

Biographers and historians have documented how kings, presidents, scientists and innovators turned to mentors, teachers or advisers for perspective. Sometimes it was a relative; at other times, a teacher, a philosopher or a strategist.

In every case, the adviser acted as a catalyst for critical thinking and moral discernment.

Even the legends and myths surrounding great leaders, enveloped in an aura of mysticism, retain this guiding figure: someone who influences without imposing, accompanies without replacing, and guides without dominating.

## 3. Intellectual synergy: when two intelligences strengthen each other

Human intelligence, conventionally measured through intelligence quotient, or IQ, varies widely. The average is around 100; a university-educated professional falls between 100 and 120; Einstein or Hawking between 160 and 170; and Marilyn vos Savant, with an IQ of 228, heads the known records.

This comparison invites us to ask:

Can an adviser with a high IQ enhance a leader's practical or emotional intelligence?

Is there an intellectual synergy that transcends the sum of two minds and produces expanded thought?

Although precise measurement would be impossible, historical examples show that collaboration between brilliant minds multiplies the possibilities for understanding and action, and that collective intelligence often exceeds individual intelligence.

## 4. From human advisers to digital advisers

Until recently, imagining a dialogue with a genius from the past — Einstein, Socrates, Mandela or Marie Curie — was an exercise in imagination. Books, letters and audiovisual records were the only ways to access their thinking.

Today, conversational artificial intelligence has turned that possibility into an everyday experience.

Through deep learning and natural-language modelling, we can assign specific roles to an AI, configure its tone, perspective and knowledge, and engage it in a dialogue of considerable intellectual depth.

For the first time, anyone can have a digital adviser on their device with intelligence equivalent to, or even greater than, that of a human genius, available at any time to analyse, suggest and broaden horizons.

## 5. Leadership facing an unpredictable future

The contemporary world is changing at an unprecedented pace. Paradigms transform before they have settled, and predicting the future has become more uncertain than ever.

In this situation, the most valuable skill will not simply be the ability to adapt, but the ability to engage intelligently with artificial intelligence.

Mastering the art of writing prompts — clear, searching and strategic questions and instructions — will be as essential as learning to write, argue or program once was.

Those who can communicate effectively with their digital advisers will achieve the same kind of synergy that once united great leaders with their human mentors.

## Conclusion

The leaders of the past relied on human advisers to think better, decide better and act more wisely.

The leaders of the future will do the same, but with digital allies able to reason, learn and accompany them in real time.

The real challenge will not be to replace human intelligence, but to learn to expand it through dialogue with our new digital advisers.
