Six months of AI with estrategIA: discoveries, dilemmas and future directions

A six-month retrospective on disinformation, models, European regulation and the AI trends identified for 2024.

Fernando Nieto LobatoAuthor
3 April 2024Originally published in Spanish
10 min readEnglish edition · 29 September 2026

This week marks six months since the first issue of estrategIA appeared on 4 October 2023, although we had previously created an issue 0. With this anniversary in mind, we used the Easter break to look back over these months and prepare a brief overview of some of the most important subjects we have covered. Many of you have joined us in recent months and probably have not read those articles. We also want to sketch how some issues have evolved during a period that, although fairly short in human terms, is significant at the speed at which AI technology is beginning to develop. A post we saw in one of Reddit's main AI communities reminded us of precisely this just a few days ago:

The problem of disinformation¶

In the first issue of estrategIA, we addressed disinformation, a problem that remains extremely serious and topical — as several news items in this very issue show — particularly in a year so full of elections. I recall that we also examined it from the perspective of Mustafa Suleyman, a co-founder of DeepMind, Google's AI laboratory, which led the field for many years. In our second issue, we took an in-depth look at his highly recommended book The Coming Wave, which had just been published in Spanish. Here, the main change has concerned the author rather than the book's contents, which remain fully relevant: barely a fortnight ago, we learned that Suleyman had left his own AI start-up, Inflection, to join Microsoft as head of a new AI division.

OpenAI, GPT-4 and its capabilities¶

In issue 3, we produced our first major practical article, exploring how GPT-4 V(ision) could analyse posters from Spain's latest general election. This was a particularly interesting application, as was the creation of agents — OpenAI's GPTs — discussed in issue 7. There has been little new in this area, and we are still waiting for OpenAI's next major model, GPT-4.5 or GPT-5, rumoured for a summer release. Despite the wait, Sam Altman said in a recent interview that it would represent a significant qualitative leap.

Meanwhile, OpenAI has been fairly inactive in releasing models publicly, though not privately. Unfortunately, this is probably strongly influenced by this year's US elections and its concerns about the misuse of extremely powerful AI tools such as Sora, which we discussed in issue 21. In recent weeks, the company has been presenting Sora privately to Hollywood studios and filmmakers. The same applies to the highly sophisticated voice-cloning system unveiled last week, which is not yet publicly accessible: Voice Engine.

The other major development at OpenAI — the company which, despite everything, continues to lead the AI revolution it popularised with ChatGPT — is that it appears to have overcome its own “Game of Thrones”, which we described in issue 8. In recent weeks, it announced a new board of directors.

What has changed, and matters greatly within the AI industry, is that GPT-4 is no longer the best language model. It has been surpassed by Claude 3's Opus version, a model we have already tested in depth in estrategIA, specifically in issue 24. Before that, we had also analysed Google's latest model, Gemini. Its Advanced version did not convince us, for several reasons, but version Pro 1.5 is becoming particularly interesting thanks to its large context window, which lets it work with substantial amounts of external information, such as whole books.

The European Union has an AI law¶

Another major development in the six months since estrategIA launched is undoubtedly the EU's progress from a draft that had been under discussion for years, with a rather uncertain future, to an agreed text finalised in December, as we reported in issue 11, and then to an AI law approved by the European Parliament, which we asked the best language models available at the time to analyse.

How the trends for 2024 are developing¶

Finally, to continue this brief review of some of the main subjects we have addressed during estrategIA's first six months, let us take a quick look at how the AI trends we identified for 2024 in issue 14 are progressing, even though only three months of the year have elapsed.

1. AI will be integrated into our everyday tools, and we will increasingly use it almost without noticing¶

This trend is still developing and has yet to take off. AI applications are certainly becoming more common on our phones, but greater integration and ease of use are still needed. Perhaps we will see major advances in this direction over the coming months — if, for example, the reports of an AI app store that Apple might soon launch are true. The same applies to autonomous agents, of which the interesting Devin is perhaps the best example so far.

2. Open-source models will narrow the gap with large proprietary commercial models still further, although the latter will remain more powerful. An open model might even match the capabilities of today's GPT-4¶

Here, progress continues to be very rapid. Last week X released Grok 1.0, and the models from the French start-up Mistral in particular are already very close to GPT-4's level. Meta's release of Llama 3 is also expected in July. It will very probably be the most powerful open-source model yet, and might well surpass GPT-4.

3. New mobile phones will have AI, in some cases even running locally¶

We have already seen what the new Samsung Galaxy S24 phones, introduced in January, and Google Pixels can do. This CNET article is worth reading if you want to explore further. The ball is now in Apple's court. Judging by the “announcement” of its Worldwide Developers Conference in June, it intends to go all out on AI with an “(A)bsolutely (I)ncredible” event. It has also just presented a very promising new AI model.

Original English screenshot, 26 March 2024. Greg Joswiak: “Mark your calendars for #WWDC24, June 10–14. It’s going to be Absolutely Incredible!” A reply from 9to5Mac reads: “(A)bsolutely (I)ncredible you say”.

4. 2024 will be the year of the great revolution in AI-generated video and audio¶

The unveiling of Sora, which we have already discussed, left everyone astonished because it demonstrated advances in video generation that had been thought to lie years ahead. Extraordinary audio tools are also being created, such as Suno.ai version 3, which we will certainly discuss in a forthcoming issue.

5. Robots¶

Anyone following AI news over recent months can see that this is an unstoppable trend, attracting billions in investment from many of the world's largest companies. Nvidia's developers' conference ended with a focus on robots, and the current landscape is fascinating. This chart, which we found on Reddit just the day before yesterday, provides an excellent overview. To see many of these leading robots in action, we recommend this article, which brings together their best demonstrations.
